Presentations

Research talks and paper explainers from the Next State AI YouTube channel. Each presentation includes a short overview and links to the paper and video.

[video] Browse the paper presentation playlist

Donghwan Lee, Han-Dong Lim, "A Switching System Theory of Q-Learning with Linear Function Approximation," [arxiv] [video] [project]

Models Q-learning with linear function approximation as a stochastic switching linear system. The joint spectral radius provides finite-time error bounds and convergence certificates that can be less conservative than bounds based on a single update.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, "Spectral Analysis of Heavy-Ball Q-value Iteration," [arxiv] [video] [project]

Studies when heavy-ball momentum can accelerate Q-value iteration. An exact switching-system representation connects convergence to the joint spectral radius and gives conditions under which the momentum method is faster than standard Q-value iteration.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, "Sign-Separated Asymmetric Finite-Time Error Analysis of Q-Learning," [arxiv] [video] [project]

Separates Q-learning errors into positive and negative components to study overestimation bias. Distinct finite-time upper bounds reveal how maximization affects the two components differently; the rate separation concerns the bounds and need not occur along every sample path.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, "Switching Theory for Q-Learning," [arxiv] [video] [project]

Develops a switching-system analysis of tabular Q-learning with a constant step size. It expresses finite-time convergence rates through the joint spectral radius and connects this quantity to deterministic-policy modes and a linear-programming characterization.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, "Beyond the Bellman Fixed Point: Geometry and Fast Policy Identification in Value Iteration," [arxiv] [video] [project]

Explains how value iteration can identify an optimal greedy policy before its value estimates reach the Bellman fixed point. A geometric switching-system analysis separates policy identification from value convergence and gives conditions for faster convergence in the policy-relevant directions.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, Hyukjun Yang, "Bellman Residual Minimization for Control: Geometry, Stationarity, and Convergence," NeurIPS 2026 [arxiv] [conference] [video] [project]

Investigates hard and soft Bellman residual objectives for control, connecting their geometry, stationary points, and descent methods. The analysis distinguishes tabular optimality and convergence guarantees from stationarity under function approximation, and examines smoothing bias and policy recovery.

[video]

Narim Jeong, Donghwan Lee*, "Finite-Time Analysis of Minimax Q-Learning," RLC 2025, RLJ 2025 [journal] [video]

Derives finite-time error bounds for minimax Q-learning and its smoothed variant in discounted two-player zero-sum games. Upper and lower comparison systems separate the effects of initialization, constant-step sampling noise, and smoothing under the stated tabular sampling assumptions.

[video]

Han-Dong Lim, Donghwan Lee*, "Finite-Time Analysis of Temporal Difference Learning with Experience Replay," Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 2024 [arxiv] [journal] [video] [project]

Quantifies how experience replay affects temporal-difference learning under Markovian observations. Finite-time bounds for averaged and final iterates show how replay-buffer capacity and mini-batch size control the error associated with a constant step size.

[video]

Han-Dong Lim, Donghwan Lee*, "Backstepping Temporal Difference Learning," ICLR 2023, Kigali, Rwanda, May 1-5, 2023 [arxiv] [conference] [video] [project]

Uses backstepping from nonlinear control to understand and design stable off-policy temporal-difference algorithms with linear function approximation. The framework unifies existing methods and motivates a new convergent algorithm, illustrated on problems where standard TD learning can diverge.

[video]

Han-Dong Lim, Donghwan Lee*, "Regularized Q-Learning," NeurIPS 2024 [arxiv] [conference] [video] [project]

Adds a suitable regularization term to stabilize Q-learning with linear function approximation. Switching-system analysis establishes convergence and bounds the error of the resulting solution, clarifying the tradeoff between stability and regularization bias.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, "Final Iteration Convergence Bound of Q-Learning: Switching System Approach," IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, vol. 69, no. 7, 2024 [arxiv] [journal] [video] [project]

Establishes a finite-time error bound for the final Q-learning iterate using a discrete-time switching-system model. The result analyzes the estimate directly used by the algorithm, without requiring an additional average of past iterates.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, Jianghai Hu, and Niao He, "A Discrete-Time Switching System Analysis of Q-Learning," SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization, vol. 61, no. 3, 2023 [arxiv] [journal] [video] [project]

Represents asynchronous Q-learning with a constant step size as a stochastic affine switching system. Upper and lower comparison dynamics yield finite-time error bounds and help explain the role of overestimation in learning.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, Han-Dong Lim, Jihoon Park, and Okyong Choi, "New Versions of Gradient Temporal-Difference Learning," IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, vol. 68, no. 8, 2023 [arxiv] [journal] [video] [project]

Introduces variants of gradient temporal-difference learning through a common convex-concave saddle-point formulation. This viewpoint connects off-policy learning with primal-dual dynamics, supports stability analysis, and enables a systematic comparison of the proposed algorithms.

[video]

Donghwan Lee, "Convergence of Dynamic Programming on the Semidefinite Cone for Discrete-Time Infinite-Horizon LQR," IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, vol. 67, no. 10, 2022 [arxiv] [journal] [video] [project]

Analyzes dynamic programming for the discrete-time infinite-horizon linear quadratic regulator using matrix inequalities and norm bounds. It establishes convergence of Q-value iteration under the stated initialization conditions and extends the analysis to policy iteration with exponential convergence guarantees.

[video]

Donghwan Lee and Niao He, "A Unified Switching System Perspective and Convergence Analysis of Q-Learning Algorithms," NeurIPS 2020 [arxiv] [conference] [video] [project]

Provides a common convergence framework for Q-learning algorithms by expressing their associated differential equations as switched linear systems. The approach covers asynchronous and averaging Q-learning and gives a sufficient convergence condition for Q-learning with linear function approximation.

[video]

Donghwan Lee and Niao He, "Periodic Q-Learning," L4DC 2020, Online, June 10–11, 2020 [arxiv] [conference] [video] [project]

Studies tabular Q-learning with separate online and periodically refreshed target estimates. Holding the target fixed connects the inner updates to stochastic optimization, enabling finite-time analysis and sample-complexity guarantees for finding a near-optimal policy.

[video]

Donghwan Lee and Niao He, "Target-Based Temporal-Difference Learning," ICML 2019, Long Beach, CA, June 11-15, 2019 [arxiv] [conference] [video] [project]

Develops temporal-difference algorithms that maintain separate online and target parameters. Averaging, double, and periodic target updates receive convergence analyses, including finite-sample results for periodic TD, providing a theoretical perspective on target networks in linear policy evaluation.
