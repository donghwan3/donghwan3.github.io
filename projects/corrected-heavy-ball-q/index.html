Heavy-Ball Q-Learning with Residual Weighting Correction

arXiv:2606.27112 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

Department of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv BibTeX

Restore a shared constant direction to identify when momentum improves Q-learning's spectral convergence rate.

Key Contributions

A structured residual correction

The correction preserves the tabular Bellman fixed point and gives every switching mode a common constant eigenvector, even with nonuniform state-action weights.

Conditional spectral improvement

A decomposition isolates the constant-direction momentum dynamics from transverse switching dynamics, yielding explicit sufficient conditions for a smaller joint spectral radius.

Linear features and sampled updates

The feature-space extension identifies its own corrected target and establishes a small-step expected-error bound for an i.i.d. stochastic implementation.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Nonuniform sampling weights obscure a common direction that makes momentum amenable to spectral analysis. This paper corrects the Bellman-residual weighting to restore that structure, then studies heavy-ball updates through exact switching linear systems. A strict gap between constant and transverse dynamics yields a conditional improvement in the joint spectral radius. The construction extends to linear features, where its target is a corrected fixed point. A separate analysis provides finite-time bounds for an i.i.d. sampled implementation under small-step and stability assumptions.

The problem

When can momentum help weighted Q-learning?

Standard weighted Q-learning need not share a constant eigenvector across policy modes. This blocks a direct transfer of spectral acceleration arguments from synchronous Q-value iteration. The paper asks whether a controlled change to residual weighting can restore that structure and support a rigorous momentum analysis, while distinguishing the tabular optimum from the target induced by linear approximation.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Correct the residual weighting

Set H = δ1dᵀ + D(I − 1dᵀ), with D = diag(d), positive sampling weights summing to one, and δ > 0. Then H1 = δ1; in the tabular case H is nonsingular and leaves Q* unchanged.

Definition 4.1 and Lemmas 4.1–4.3, pp. 7–8

Separate constant and transverse dynamics

Augment the error state with its previous value and decompose the switching family. The update uses momentum coefficient αη. For linear features, a representable constant feature produces an analogous decomposition around the unique corrected fixed point.

Sections 5 and 10, pp. 12–16 and 24–30

Qk+1 = Qk + αH[F(Qk) − Qk] + αη(Qk − Qk−1)

The deterministic corrected heavy-ball update. F is the Bellman optimality map; H is the residual-weighting correction; the actual momentum coefficient is αη.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

Momentum improves the corrected mean recursion under a gap

If 0 < 1 − αδ(1 − γ) < 1 and the transverse joint spectral radius is strictly smaller, sufficiently small positive αη gives a strictly smaller full joint spectral radius than corrected Q-learning without momentum.

Theorem 5.1, p. 16

Comparison with ordinary Q-learning needs extra conditions

The strict comparison also holds against standard deterministic Q-learning when 0 < αd(s,a) ≤ 1 and d_max ≤ δ < 1/[α(1 − γ)], together with the strict transverse gap and admissible small momentum.

Corollary 6.1, pp. 18–19

A finite-time stochastic guarantee

With a unique corrected feature-space fixed point, fixed problem and momentum parameters, a stable reference corrected switching family, and sufficiently small α, the i.i.d. sampled recursion has decaying transients plus an O(√α) expected-error residual for fixed initialization.

Theorem 12.1 and Assumptions 12.1–12.2, pp. 34–41

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

The acceleration statements are conditional, not a universal benefit of momentum or residual correction. Joint-spectral-radius stability is a sufficient certificate for the state-dependent recursion. In linear approximation, the corrected fixed point generally differs from both Q* and the ordinary projected Bellman solution. The stochastic result assumes i.i.d. sampling and small constant step size; its residual bound does not establish exact convergence at fixed α. For fixed η, the small-α improvement is second order rather than a leading-order acceleration.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
