Switching-Geometry Analysis of Deflated Q-Value Iteration

arXiv:2605.10811 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

Department of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv BibTeX

Remove the slow constant shift in value error while preserving the greedy-policy sequence.

Key Contributions

A precise geometric rate decomposition

The analysis identifies the all-ones direction responsible for the sharp worst-case γ rate and isolates the transverse switching dynamics.

An exact deflated-family rate

Removing the autonomous constant mode makes the full deflated switching-family joint spectral radius equal to the projected radius, with a finite-time full-error estimate.

A clear interpretation of the correction

Scalar-shift equivalence proves that deflation changes full value-error convergence while leaving the projected path and greedy decisions unchanged under matching initialization and tie-breaking.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

A uniform shift of every action value changes numerical error without changing greedy decisions. This paper separates that constant direction from the remaining dynamics of discounted Q-value iteration. Standard iteration has joint spectral radius exactly γ, while a rank-one residual correction removes the autonomous constant mode. The corrected family's rate becomes the projected joint spectral radius, which is no larger than γ and can be smaller. Crucially, the corrected trajectory differs from ordinary iteration only by scalar shifts, so matching initialization preserves the greedy-policy sequence.

The problem

Which part of value error is slowing down?

Standard Q-value iteration can retain a slowly decaying common offset even after its action preferences are accurate. A full-vector error bound mixes that offset with policy-relevant differences. The paper asks how a rank-one residual correction changes this geometry, which convergence rate it can improve, and whether its numerical gains imply a different sequence of greedy policies.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Split constant and transverse errors

Project the error onto the subspace orthogonal to the all-ones vector. Every Bellman switching mode preserves the constant direction with eigenvalue γ, while the projected family has joint spectral radius ρ̄ ≤ γ.

Propositions 1–2 and Lemmas 6–7, pp. 8–10

Apply a scalar residual correction

Add γ/(1 − γ) times the residual average in the all-ones direction. The augmented error matrix has a zero autonomous scalar block. Any fixed probability distribution may replace the uniform residual average without changing the associated projected rate.

Eq. (7), Proposition 3 and Theorem 3, pp. 10–17

Qk+1 = F(Qk) + [γ/(1 − γ)]dT[F(Qk) − Qk]1

F is the discounted Bellman optimality map, 1 is the all-ones vector, and d is a fixed probability distribution over state-action pairs. Uniform d gives the empirical-mean residual correction.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

A sharper full-error certificate

The deflated switching-family radius equals ρ̄. For any ε > 0 with ρ̄ + ε < 1, Theorem 2 bounds the full error at k ≥ 1 by a constant times (ρ̄ + ε)^(k−1) times the initial projected error. Strict improvement over the classical γ rate requires ρ̄ < γ.

Theorems 1–2, pp. 13–14

No faster policy identification from recentering alone

For identical initial Q-vectors, the corrected and ordinary iterates differ only by a scalar multiple of the all-ones vector. Their projected trajectories and tie-broken greedy policies therefore coincide at every iteration.

Lemma 9, p. 11

An exact example separates the rates

In a one-state, two-action MDP, every projected mode is zero. Thus the projected and deflated-family radii are zero, whereas the standard full-family radius is γ for every 0 < γ < 1. Numerical illustrations also examine full value error on FrozenLake and Taxi-v3.

Example 1 and Section 11, pp. 13–19

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

The result concerns deterministic Bellman updates in finite discounted MDPs and a fixed residual-averaging distribution. It does not establish guarantees for adaptively changing weights or sampled Q-learning. The improved rate is a full-value-error certificate; the projected trajectory and greedy-policy sequence are unchanged from standard iteration with the same initialization. Strict improvement requires a projected radius below γ, and the family rate can be conservative for a particular trajectory. Numerical illustrations do not establish a universal wall-clock speedup.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
