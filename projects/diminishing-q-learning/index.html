Finite-Time Analysis of Asynchronous Q-learning under Diminishing Step-Size from Control-Theoretic View

arXiv:2207.12217 · 2022

Han-Dong Lim, Donghwan Lee

Department of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv Journal BibTeX

A time-varying switching-system analysis bounds the final Q-learning error under diminishing step sizes and Markovian observations.

Key Contributions

A diminishing-step switching model

The analysis extends the control-system viewpoint to time-varying learning rates and bounds the original final iterate, without requiring an average of the Q estimates.

Coordinates suited to stability analysis

A Lyapunov-matrix similarity transformation converts the lower comparison dynamics into a form with a negative-definite symmetric part, enabling explicit recursive error estimates.

An explicit treatment of dependence

The Markovian analysis traces how mixing affects the comparison-system errors. Its step-size conditions avoid an explicit mixing-time parameter, while the resulting error bounds still depend on mixing.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

This paper studies how quickly asynchronous tabular Q-learning approaches its optimal action values when observations come from a Markov chain. It represents the learning recursion as a stochastic switching system whose matrices change with both the greedy policy and a diminishing step size. Upper and lower comparison systems make that nonlinear recursion easier to bound. A similarity transformation supports the analysis of the resulting time-varying dynamics. The final-iterate guarantee separates an independent-sampling baseline from the extra contribution of Markovian dependence, producing a logarithmically modified inverse-square-root rate under the stated assumptions.

The problem

Quantifying learning from a trajectory

Asymptotic convergence does not tell us the error after a finite number of updates. For asynchronous Q-learning, only the observed state-action entry changes, while maximization changes the active system matrix. Diminishing learning rates add time variation, and trajectory samples introduce temporal dependence. The challenge is to quantify their combined effect on the final estimate under an exploration and step-size model.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Bound the nonlinear recursion

Construct lower and upper comparison systems around the optimal Q-function. Their trajectories enclose the original update componentwise; analyzing the lower system and the gap between the bounds avoids directly solving the nonlinear switching recursion.

Sections 3–5, pp. 12–26

Transform, then account for mixing

Choose a reciprocal diminishing step size with coefficients satisfying the paper's spectral conditions. A similarity transformation handles the lower dynamics, and delayed-noise estimates extend the independent-sampling analysis to an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

Independent-sampling baseline

Theorem 6.1 bounds the expected sup-norm error of the final iterate by an inverse-square-root term under the prescribed step-size rule. Constants depend on discounting, visitation probabilities, and state-action dimension.

Theorem 6.1, p. 27

A Markovian final-iterate bound

After the specified mixing threshold, Theorem 7.3 bounds expected error with an additional mixing contribution. Remark 7.1 yields order sqrt(log k / k); this is an expectation guarantee, not a deterministic trajectory bound.

Theorem 7.3 and Remark 7.1, pp. 39–40

A rate with explicit qualifications

Avoiding mixing time in the step-size rule does not remove mixing from the analysis. The paper explicitly identifies potentially poor sample-complexity dependence, positioning the method as a complementary analytical framework.

Remark 7.1 and Section 7.4, p. 40

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

The results concern finite discounted tabular MDPs, bounded rewards and initialization, and a fixed behavior policy with adequate coverage. The Markovian model assumes an irreducible, aperiodic state-action chain. The reciprocal step-size coefficients must satisfy the paper's conditions; an arbitrary decreasing schedule is not covered. Although the iteration rate has a simple form, its constants and mixing threshold can be unfavorable. The paper does not establish guarantees for neural approximation, adaptive exploration, or improved empirical performance.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
