Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Agents observe local rewards but seek the optimal value for their average reward. This paper studies a tabular consensus-plus-innovation update through a network disagreement term and an optimality term. Comparison systems yield finite-time guarantees for independent and Markovian observations.

The problem

Why this problem matters

Local rewards do not reveal the global objective. Communication must spread information while nonlinear Bellman updates and sampling noise continue to evolve.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Combine consensus and local updates

Each agent mixes neighboring Q-tables and adds a local TD correction. The symmetric doubly stochastic communication matrix has positive self-weights; the constant step size is bounded by those weights.

Sandwich the average dynamics

A lower linear system and an upper switching system bound the network average. Their difference removes shared noise, allowing consensus and optimality estimates to be combined.

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

The guarantees concern finite discounted MDPs, bounded rewards, full state-action coverage and the stated connected communication model. A fixed step size leaves a residual bound; the paper does not establish optimal sample complexity or neural-network convergence.
