Spectral Analysis of Dueling Q-Learning

arXiv:2607.08340 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

Department of Electrical Engineering, Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv BibTeX

Separate common and differential action values to reveal the stability of tabular dueling Q-learning.

Key Contributions

A centered decomposition

Represent each Q-vector uniquely as its state-wise action average plus a zero-mean action-differential component. These are coordinates of Q, rather than automatically conventional policy-dependent values and advantages.

Equivalent learning descriptions

Prove that centered value–advantage updates and a Q-only update generate identical iterates. Their switching representations have the same joint spectral radius, so changing coordinates introduces no extra stability conservatism.

Finite-time stochastic analysis

Treat the unregularized, unprojected sampled recursion under i.i.d. observations. Reference-gain stability and a sufficiently small common gain yield bounded second moments and a shrinking expected-error residual bound.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Dueling architectures motivate separate value and advantage updates, but their tabular learning dynamics need a precise stability account. This paper studies a centered, unregularized dueling recursion and shows that it is exactly a preconditioned Q-learning update. Orthogonal action-common and action-differential coordinates reveal how its two gains act. A switching-system representation yields deterministic convergence certificates through the joint spectral radius. For independent samples and sufficiently small gains in a fixed ratio, the analysis also bounds expected error without projecting the iterates. A simple deterministic example illustrates the rate separation.

The problem

What does the dueling decomposition change?

Separating a state value from action advantages changes how a temporal-difference residual is shared across actions. The resulting updates are coupled, and their behavior is not explained by analyzing two independent learners. The problem is to identify the exact tabular recursion, its relationship to ordinary Q-learning, and conditions that control both deterministic switching and stochastic sampling errors.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Give two subspaces different gains

The preconditioner is C = |A|αΠ + βΠ⊥, where Π averages actions within each state. Equal effective gains |A|α = β recover standard Q-learning; other choices change the relative common and differential dynamics.

Section 5.3, Eqs. 19–20, PDF p. 15

Study the switched error system

Bellman differences give exact policy-indexed modes I − CD(I − γPΓμ). Stochastic-policy modes lie in the convex hull of deterministic modes, allowing a finite-family JSR certificate to control the nonlinear mean recursion.

Lemma 5.7, PDF pp. 16–17

Qk+1 = Qk + (|𝒜|αΠ + βΠ⊥)D(F(Qk) − Qk)

The deterministic Q-only recursion applies separate gains to the action-common and action-differential parts of the residual (Eq. 19).

Main results

What the analysis establishes

Deterministic convergence to Q*

A JSR below one is sufficient for global convergence of the tabular mean recursion to the optimal Q-function, with a geometric norm bound at any rate slightly above that JSR. An explicit conservative gain interval is also supplied.

Proposition 5.1 and Lemma 5.9, PDF pp. 18–20

An expected-error bound for small gains

Under i.i.d. sampling, stable reference gains, fixed gain ratio β/α, and α no larger than the Appendix D.6 threshold, Theorem 6.1 gives decaying transients plus an O(√α) residual upper bound for fixed initialization.

Theorem 6.1; Lemma D.6, PDF pp. 26 and 40

An illustrative rate separation

The one-state, two-action example uses γ = 0.9 and η = α = β = 0.2, with Q* = (9, 10). Its common component benefits from a different gain; the comparison is a deterministic illustration, not a general empirical speedup claim.

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

The analysis concerns a centered tabular algorithm, not the nonlinear neural-network dueling DQN architecture. The components remain coupled in general MDPs. JSR stability is a sufficient certificate; failure of that certificate is not itself a proof of divergence along the Bellman-generated path. The stochastic theorem requires i.i.d. observations, a stable reference gain pair, fixed gain ratio, and sufficiently small gains. Its residual is an upper bound, not a claim of zero error at a fixed nonzero gain or an unavoidable positive error floor.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
