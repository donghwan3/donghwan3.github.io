Geometrically Averaged Hard Target Updates for Linear Q-Learning

arXiv:2606.10835 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

School of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, Republic of Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv BibTeX

A continuous target-period parameter connects one-step linear Q-learning with projected Q-value iteration.

Key Contributions

Continuous target-period interpolation

Geometric weighting turns discrete target-copy periods into a continuous family while preserving the projected Bellman fixed-point equation.

Computable implementations

An exact linear correction replaces the conceptual infinite sum. An inverse-free auxiliary iteration tracks the same correction equation with different augmented dynamics.

Separate stability guarantees

Endpoint, inverse-free, and random-period results explicitly use the switching family appropriate to each implementation.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Rather than select one fixed target-copy period, this work averages hard-target boundary maps using geometric weights. The resulting λ-target family connects ordinary deterministic linear Q-learning at λ = 0 with projected Q-value iteration as λ approaches one. Exact switching-system models explain how stability certificates transfer near these endpoints. A closed-form correction computes the infinite average through one linear solve, while an auxiliary recursion avoids the inverse. A separate random-period implementation admits pathwise and mean-square bounds under a stronger uniform switching condition.

The problem

What changes when target periods are averaged?

Target networks introduce a discrete choice of how many online updates occur before a copy. The paper asks how to vary that choice continuously and identify the resulting stability mechanism in linear Q-learning. Its central object is the map between target boundaries, where the frozen target and its greedy policy remain fixed during each candidate inner period.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Average frozen-target boundary maps

Weight the period-m candidate by (1 − λ)λ^(m−1), for 0 ≤ λ < 1. The continuous λ = 1 endpoint is defined by the limiting projected Q-value iteration map, not by a geometric distribution.

Section 7, Eq. (17) and Lemma 7.4, pp. 11–15

Solve or track one correction equation

The matrix (1 − λ)I + λαΦᵀDΦ converts a projected Bellman residual into the exact averaged correction. Richardson updates provide an inverse-free implementation; simultaneous target movement changes its switching dynamics.

Proposition 8.1 and Section 9, pp. 16–24

wm = (1 − λ)λm−1,   m ≥ 1,   0 ≤ λ < 1

The weights average hard-target boundary maps. λ = 0 selects one inner step; λ approaching one yields the continuous projected Q-value iteration endpoint under the inner-step stability condition.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

Endpoint stability transfers locally

With full-rank features, positive weighting and 0 < α < 2/λ_max(ΦᵀDΦ), λ near zero inherits the strict joint-spectral-radius status of ordinary linear Q-learning; λ near one inherits that of projected Q-value iteration. This is a neighborhood result.

Theorem 7.1, p. 15

An inverse-free stability result

If the inverse-form switching family is stable and the auxiliary Richardson step satisfies its eigenvalue bound, sufficiently small positive boundary step β makes the augmented two-line recursion stable. At β = 0 its joint spectral radius equals one.

Theorem 9.1, p. 24

Random periods with a uniform certificate

If the union of all deterministic-policy, finite-period boundary matrices has joint spectral radius below one, the random-period recursion obeys a geometric squared-error bound, pathwise and in expectation, around the assumed unique projected Bellman solution.

Theorem 10.1, p. 26

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

Geometric averaging concerns target-copy periods, not multi-step reward returns or eligibility traces. Stable endpoints do not certify every intermediate λ, and a joint spectral radius above one does not prove divergence of a realized greedy trajectory. The random-period theorem randomizes the period while retaining model-based inner updates; it is not a finite-sample theorem for noisy transition data. Its uniform all-period condition is stronger than stability of the averaged map. The analysis concerns linear features, not nonlinear deep-network training.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
