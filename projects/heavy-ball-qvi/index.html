Spectral Analysis of Heavy-Ball Q-value Iteration

arXiv:2607.22627 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

Department of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv Video BibTeX

A shared constant mode and a transverse spectral gap explain when momentum accelerates Q-value iteration.

Key Contributions

An exact augmented switching model

Stacking consecutive Q-errors captures momentum and Bellman policy switching while retaining the optimal Bellman fixed point.

Necessary and sufficient ingredients kept separate

Improving the constant-direction block supplies a necessary condition for full-family acceleration. A strict transverse gap and sufficiently small positive momentum give a sufficient full-family result.

A projected linear-feature extension

When the feature space contains constants, the same decomposition yields a conditional small-momentum improvement for projected Q-value iteration with full-rank features and positive weighting.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Adding heavy-ball momentum to Q-value iteration creates a switching system with memory, so a single fixed-policy eigenvalue calculation cannot establish global acceleration. This work derives the exact augmented error dynamics and separates a shared constant-direction block from the policy-dependent transverse family. It identifies an exact improvement interval for the constant block and a sufficient spectral-gap condition for improvement of the full joint spectral radius. An appendix extends the construction to projected iteration with linear features, and finite-state examples illustrate numerical value-error reductions.

The problem

Can a scalar momentum gain improve a switching process?

Greedy actions can change as Q-value iteration progresses, so its error follows a family of matrices rather than one fixed linear model. Heavy-ball momentum adds a lagged state and changes both constant and transverse dynamics. The paper asks which spectral conditions certify a smaller worst-case convergence factor, beyond an improvement seen for one policy or one direction.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Augment with the previous error

Use the recursion Q_next = Q + α(F(Q) − Q) + η(Q − Q_previous), with 0 < α ≤ 1. Bellman-difference selectors give an exact switching representation whose fixed point is (Q*, Q*).

Section 5, Eqs. (5.1)–(5.4), p. 5

Separate two spectral contributions

The constant direction becomes a two-dimensional companion block. The full joint spectral radius is the maximum of that block's spectral radius and the projected heavy-ball family's joint spectral radius.

Lemmas 5.3–5.4 and 6.2, pp. 5–6

Qk+1 = Qk + α[F(Qk) − Qk] + η(Qk − Qk−1)

The deterministic heavy-ball Q-value iteration update. F is the Bellman optimality map, α is the relaxation step, and η is the momentum coefficient. Here the coefficient is η, unlike the αη convention in the residual-correction paper.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

The constant-mode interval is necessary, not sufficient

Writing r = 1 − α(1 − γ), the constant companion block improves on standard relaxed Q-value iteration exactly when 0 < η < r². Full-family improvement additionally requires control of the transverse switching dynamics.

Proposition 5.1, p. 5

A strict gap yields full-family improvement

If the projected standard family has joint spectral radius strictly below r, there exists η₀ > 0 such that 0 < η < min(r², η₀) gives a heavy-ball joint spectral radius below r and global exponential convergence of the deterministic error.

Theorem 6.1, p. 6

A small MDP illustrates the numerical effect

For the reported two-state, two-action example with γ = 0.95, α = 1, η = 0.60 and zero initialization, the sup-norm error at iteration 120 is approximately 2.779 × 10⁻² for standard Q-value iteration and 1.211 × 10⁻¹⁰ for heavy-ball iteration.

Section 7 and Figure 1, p. 7

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

Momentum is not uniformly beneficial. The exact constant-mode interval alone does not control the transverse family, and the theorem's positive momentum threshold depends on a strict projected spectral gap. Joint spectral radius measures worst-case switching products, so an individual trajectory may converge faster than its certificate. The analysis is deterministic and model based; nonuniform coordinate sampling can destroy the common eigenvector structure and requires separate stochastic analysis. The numerical comparison is one finite-state example, not a general speedup guarantee.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
