On the Local Quadratic Stability of T-S Fuzzy Systems in the Vicinity of the Origin

arXiv:2309.06841 · 2023

Donghwan Lee, Do Wan Kim

Department of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, South Korea
Department of Electrical Engineering, Hanbat National University, Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv Journal BibTeX

Membership values at the equilibrium enable local quadratic stability tests that exploit a fuzzy system's nearby linear structure.

Key Contributions

Use equilibrium-specific information

The tests incorporate membership values at the origin, retaining local structure that conditions based only on subsystem matrices can discard. Membership continuity suffices for the basic local quadratic approach.

Provide alternative LMI constructions

Polytopic and ball bounds on membership deviations yield three quadratic stability tests with different computational costs. Their feasible Lyapunov sublevel sets provide certified local attraction regions.

State converse results and limits

The analysis distinguishes sufficient local certificates from conditional necessary-and-sufficient characterizations of exponential stability, and shows why the discussed tests cannot certify certain non-exponentially stable systems.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Takagi-Sugeno models express nonlinear dynamics as membership-weighted linear subsystems. Requiring a single quadratic Lyapunov function to decrease for every subsystem can be unnecessarily restrictive near an equilibrium. This paper instead uses the membership values at the origin to identify a nominal linear system, then bounds deviations from those values. Several resulting LMI tests certify local stability. Converse theorems characterize local exponential stability under additional smoothness assumptions and sufficiently small deviation bounds. The paper also explains limits of quadratic and fuzzy Lyapunov tests and combines the two approaches to improve feasibility.

The problem

Certifying stability where it matters

A nonlinear system can be stable close to the origin even when a global requirement across its fuzzy subsystem matrices is infeasible. Membership functions determine which combinations occur nearby. The challenge is to use that information in tractable stability tests, while keeping a clear distinction between proving decay in a local region and guaranteeing behavior throughout the entire modeling domain.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Separate nominal dynamics and variation

Evaluate membership functions at the origin to form a nominal matrix. Write the remaining dynamics using membership deviations, which become small near the origin when the memberships are continuous, and analyze a quadratic Lyapunov candidate.

Section III and equation (6), pp. 3–4

Certify a bounded neighborhood

Bound the deviation vector by a hyperrectangle or a ball and derive finite LMIs. Select a Lyapunov sublevel set contained in the corresponding valid region. A later construction combines quadratic and fuzzy Lyapunov terms.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

Sufficient local certificates

Theorems 3, 5, and 6 provide sufficient LMI conditions under continuous membership functions. The certified attraction region is a suitable Lyapunov sublevel set inside the region where the chosen membership-variation bounds hold.

Theorems 3, 5 and 6, pp. 4–6

Conditional converse guarantees

Theorems 8 and 9 give equivalence with local exponential stability when memberships are differentiable, the nonlinear vector field is continuously differentiable near the origin, and deviation bounds can be chosen sufficiently small. This is not equivalence for arbitrary fixed bounds.

Theorems 8–9, p. 7

Feasibility and attraction-region examples

Numerical examples compare feasible parameter sets and attraction estimates. Theorems 10 and 15 supply distinct feasibility-inclusion statements; the text cautions that comparisons with fixed hyperparameters need not have the same ordering.

Examples 5–7; Theorems 10 and 15, pp. 5–9

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

These are local, continuous-time stability results, not global guarantees or controller-synthesis results. A certified sublevel set must stay inside the modeling and membership-bound region. The converse results require stronger smoothness than the basic sufficient tests and allow the neighborhood bounds to shrink. Smaller bounds can reduce conservatism while shrinking the certified region. The discussed strict quadratic and fuzzy Lyapunov LMI tests cannot certify equilibria that are asymptotically stable but not exponentially stable. Discrete-time and feedback-design extensions remain future work.
