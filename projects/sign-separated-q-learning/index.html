Sign-Separated Asymmetric Finite-Time Error Analysis of Q-Learning

arXiv:2605.16103 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

Department of Electrical Engineering, Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), Daejeon, South Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv Video BibTeX

Separating positive and negative value errors reveals how maximization can create asymmetric learning dynamics.

Key Contributions

A sign-aware error decomposition

The analysis retains the direction of each value-estimation error, exposing structure that a single absolute-error estimate would conceal. Positive and negative components receive separate comparison systems.

Different spectral certificates

Negative errors are bounded using the best fixed optimal-policy mode. Positive errors require the full deterministic-policy switching family, whose joint spectral radius can produce a slower certificate.

Stochastic and deterministic guarantees

The paper distinguishes noiseless bounds from expected-error bounds under independent sampling. The latter explicitly retain the residual error caused by using a constant learning rate.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

A single error norm can hide an important distinction in Q-learning: estimates above the optimal value and estimates below it need not admit the same convergence certificate. This paper separates these components and studies them through nonnegative comparison systems. An optimal-policy linear system controls negative errors, while a larger switching family controls positive errors. The analysis derives deterministic and stochastic finite-time bounds, explains a possible separation of exponential rates, and keeps constant-step-size noise effects explicit. The conclusions concern certified bounds rather than a universal ordering of observed errors.

The problem

What does overestimation change in convergence theory?

Maximization can select an action because its estimate contains a positive error. Standard convergence bounds often measure only the largest absolute error, losing this directional information. The problem is to identify how the Bellman maximum treats errors of opposite signs, while avoiding the stronger and generally unjustified claim that every sample trajectory must remain positively biased or exhibit a fixed rate ordering.

From problem to solution

How the method works

Construct two reference systems

Represent the Bellman-max error using a stochastic policy and preserve the original martingale noise. Compare the resulting recursion with a fixed optimal-policy linear filter below and a predictable switching filter above.

Section 3.1 and Lemma 10, pp. 5-6

Translate comparison dynamics into bounds

Use matrix-product growth and spectral radii to obtain separate transient envelopes. Noise estimates rely on finite state and action spaces, bounded rewards, positive sampling probability for every pair, and a constant step size between zero and one.

Section 3.2, Theorems 13 and 15, pp. 5-6

The positive and negative parts are componentwise nonnegative. Their separate magnitudes identify overestimation and underestimation without asserting either sign dominates.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

A possible asymmetry in certified rates

The optimized negative-side spectral rate is no larger than the full-family positive-side rate. A strict inequality supports a faster negative-error certificate; it does not prove that every realized trajectory exhibits that separation.

Theorems 13 and 15, pp. 5-6

A constant-step-size residual bound

Both stochastic estimates include a constant-step-size residual upper bound. The positive-side bound also contains a polynomial transient factor. These terms matter when interpreting finite-time performance and prevent a general claim of exact convergence at a fixed step size.

Theorem 15 and discussion, p. 6

Deterministic bounds and an illustration

The noiseless counterpart admits separate exponential bounds without stochastic residual terms. The paper also illustrates sign-separated errors on Sutton's maximization-bias example; that illustration is evidence for possible behavior, not a universal empirical guarantee.

Appendix A.3, Theorems 7-8; Figure 1, p. 1

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

The main stochastic analysis assumes independent observations and full state-action coverage; it does not establish the same bounds for arbitrary trajectory-dependent sampling. Results concern finite tabular discounted problems rather than neural-network Q-functions. Spectral rates describe comparison certificates, with prefactors and residual upper bounds still affecting practical behavior. In particular, the theory does not imply a nonnegative expected Q-error for every problem, nor that negative errors always disappear faster on individual sample paths.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
