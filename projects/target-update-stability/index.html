Target Updates May Stabilize Linear Q-Learning: Periodic and Soft Dynamics

arXiv:2606.02645 · 2026

Donghwan Lee

School of Electrical Engineering, KAIST, Daejeon, Republic of Korea

Paper (PDF) arXiv BibTeX

Exact switching dynamics explain when hard and soft targets stabilize deterministic linear Q-learning.

Key Contributions

Exact target-boundary dynamics

A closed-form period map connects one-step linear Q-learning with projected Q-value iteration and identifies the matrix family that governs hard-target stability.

Two distinct soft-target systems

Updating the target from the new online parameter and updating it from the old online parameter produce different augmented dynamics, particularly at large target gains.

Conditional exact convergence

Joint-spectral-radius certificates yield convergence of the deterministic iterates to the projected Bellman solution without adding regularization or a residual-neighborhood target.

Abstract

A summary of the paper, written for this page.

Target networks change the feedback dynamics of bootstrapped learning. This paper derives exact switching linear systems for periodic hard targets and two soft-target update orderings in deterministic linear Q-learning. Under an explicit online step-size range, long hard-target periods approach projected Q-value iteration. Stability of that endpoint also certifies sufficiently slow, positively moving soft targets. The analysis separates update orderings, distinguishes endpoint certificates from intermediate regimes, and establishes convergence to the projected Bellman solution when the stated spectral conditions hold.

The problem

Why can a target network change stability?

Plain linear Q-learning can fail to converge, and reducing its online step size does not fully describe the effect of a target network. The paper asks which feedback dynamics hard copies and soft averaging actually create. It compares their exact switching families and identifies conditions under which a target mechanism reaches the same projected Bellman solution.

Main results

What the analysis establishes

Long hard-target periods inherit a stable endpoint

With full-rank features, positive weighting and 0 < α < 2/λ_max(ΦᵀDΦ), a projected-Q-value-iteration joint spectral radius below one certifies every sufficiently large target period. The period-one endpoint exactly matches ordinary deterministic linear Q-learning.

Theorem 6.1, p. 16

Slow positive soft updates can stabilize both orderings

Under the same online step-size condition and a stable projected-Q-value-iteration family, both soft-target orderings are stable for sufficiently small positive target gain β. Exactly β = 0 freezes the target and has joint spectral radius one.

Theorems 7.1 and 8.1, pp. 19 and 22

The large-gain endpoint depends on ordering

When ordinary deterministic linear Q-learning is stable, the after-online-update target rule is stable for β sufficiently close to one. The before-online-update rule retains a delayed coordinate at β = 1, so that conclusion does not transfer automatically.

Theorem 7.2 and discussion following Theorem 8.1, pp. 19–22

Reading the guarantees

Assumptions and scope

These are conditional results for deterministic linear approximation. Transition-sampling noise requires additional analysis; the paper does not establish a stochastic finite-sample guarantee. Joint spectral radius below one is sufficient, while a value above one does not establish divergence of every realized trajectory. Endpoint certificates do not cover every intermediate target period or gain. Hard-target rates measured per boundary should not be interpreted as per-inner-step speedups. The projected Bellman solution need not represent the exact optimal Q-function.

See the reviewed paper version for complete hypotheses, constants, and proofs.
